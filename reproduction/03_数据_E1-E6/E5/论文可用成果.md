## English manuscript-ready paragraphs

### Methods: separating client validation from model evaluation

We evaluated the system at two distinct levels. E2 tested whether the BEST client satisfied its protocol and parsing contracts on frozen inputs, whereas E5 evaluated model decisions and tool use under matched frozen domain responses. These experiments addressed different questions: deterministic client correctness did not establish the success of natural-language scientific tasks.

For E2, we froze 21 scenarios and executed three repetitions per scenario using fresh clients and transport sessions. The production BestClient followed its normal query and parsing paths through a query-aware local transport. The transport reconstructed responses from frozen rows where required by the scenario query. Scenario definitions, snapshots, automatically generated gold data and designated code files were bound by SHA-256 manifests. An independent oracle verification passed all 21 cases without importing the system-under-test modules. The evaluation covered row sets, fields, units and precision, pagination and truncation, result states, version identity, and determinism and request counts; inapplicable components were retained as such rather than assigned fabricated observations.

For E5, we retained the original 56 tasks and fixed follow-up turns, evaluating 12 arms with three fresh-session repetitions per task and arm, for 2,016 units. The runtime was Harness CLI 0.2.0-rc.2 with a mounted, restricted Claude Science preset and opencode-go/deepseek-v4.1-flash. Model decisions and tool executions were genuine Harness events; the domain transport used recaptured public responses matched to the requested catalog, coordinates and radius. Each unit had a fixed 4,096-token setting and a 600-second time limit. Unknown sampling parameters, model revision identifiers and billed costs were not imputed. The Harness executor and scorer used separate Windows identities, with gold material inaccessible to the executor. Scores were produced only after complete evidence sealing. Documented storage compatibility adapters decoded native JSON argument strings and enumerated absent worker-receipt directories without changing event values or task-scoring predicates.

The primary E5 estimand was the mean task-paired difference between B3 and B2b. Three binary rule scores were first averaged within each task and arm, and the 56 tasks formed the paired units. We used an exact two-sided sign-flip test and 10,000 paired task bootstrap resamples. Six comparisons of B3 with A1–A6 formed a separate Holm family, and A2 versus sham was reported separately. A task-random-intercept Bayesian logistic model was a prespecified sensitivity analysis and did not replace the primary test. Category-specific results and the subsequent failure diagnosis were descriptive.

### Results: E2

The accepted E2 run completed all 63 unique scenario–repetition combinations, and all 21 scenarios passed the frozen contract rubric. Each scenario had an applicability-adjusted weighted score of 1.00, and the counterexample-detection metascore was 1.00. Output determinism held across the three repetitions. The evidence included an empirical PS1 field captured on 4 October 2026, containing 1,000 rows within a radius of 1,800 arcseconds, together with explicitly synthetic stress fixtures for 60,000 rows and NULL-versus-zero distinctions. These findings support client behavior on the specified frozen inputs; they do not establish current live-service availability, performance in a globular-cluster core, or expert validation of every astronomical gold value.

### Results: E5

All 2,016 planned E5 units were executed and sealed with unique source sessions. B3 passed the mechanical rubric in 95 of 168 units (56.55%), compared with 83 of 168 for B2b (49.40%). The task-paired difference was 7.14 percentage points, with a 95% task-bootstrap interval from 0.00 to 14.88 percentage points and an exact two-sided p-value of 0.08848. The prespecified primary test therefore did not reject the null at the 0.05 level. This result neither establishes an incremental advantage nor demonstrates equivalence between the arms. None of the six ablation comparisons was significant after Holm correction; the A2–sham difference was zero, with p = 1.00.

The aggregate difference masked variation across task classes. B3 and B2b passed 19/33 and 22/33 data-task units, 8/21 and 6/21 clarification units, 63/96 and 53/96 refusal units, and 5/18 and 2/18 semantic units, respectively. These category results were descriptive and did not constitute separate confirmatory findings. Across the 56 tasks, B3 had a higher three-repetition mean on 13 tasks, a lower mean on six, and the same mean on 37. The overall net difference was 12 additional rule-passing units. A total of 236 units reached the fixed token budget and remained failures under the original rubric; they were neither rerun nor excluded.

### Discussion

The experiments provide different strengths of evidence. E2 supplies reproducible evidence for the specified client contracts, including pagination and missing-value boundary cases. E5 supplies an auditable model-evaluation dataset but leaves the incremental benefit of extra guidance unresolved at the prespecified threshold. The primary estimate is compatible with a range from no aggregate gain to a potentially useful gain under the task-bootstrap procedure, rather than a precisely established improvement.

Several design properties help contextualize this uncertainty. Both B3 and B2b shared typed tool schemas, neutral interface documentation and hard execution boundaries, so the primary comparison estimated the addition of guidance to an already informative interface. Many tasks yielded equal outcomes, and gains in refusal and semantic tasks were partly offset by lower data-task performance. Token-budget termination constrained the attainable scores, but occurred equally often in the two primary arms (23/168 each); without a budget intervention, it cannot be identified as the cause of the nonsignificant contrast. Mechanical JSON, reference and keyword predicates may also disagree with human judgments, which remain pending. Finally, A2 retained shared schema semantics, sham matched characters rather than verified model tokens, and A6 jointly changed guidance and layout. These limits prevent stronger claims about isolated semantic, length or layout mechanisms.
